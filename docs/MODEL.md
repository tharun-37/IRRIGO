# Model

Eight estimators, trained on a physically simulated corpus driven by real weather. This
document covers what is modelled, how the data is produced, and what the measured results
do and do not support.

---

## The estimators

| Estimator | Task | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Disease risk | Classification, 6 classes | Stress-risk label and distribution |
| Should irrigate | Binary classification | Probability, thresholded at 0.13 |
| Water depth | Regression, on irrigation events | Millimetres to apply |
| Irrigation action | Classification, 4 classes | None, light, moderate, heavy |
| Nutrient demand | Three regressions | N, P, K in kg/ha |
| Yield | Regression | Tonnes per hectare |

All are `HistGradientBoosting` estimators, which handle the nonlinear interactions between
soil, crop and weather without the preprocessing a neural model would need, and predict in
under a millisecond on a CPU.

### Why the classifier is tuned toward watering

The decision threshold is deliberately low. The cost of the two error types is not
symmetric: a missed irrigation costs a crop, an unnecessary one costs a few litres. At a
balanced threshold the classifier missed 103 events in the last evaluation and triggered 190
unnecessary ones. The tuned threshold accepts more false positives in exchange for
recalling more real stress.

This is a policy decision, not a modelling result, and it is worth revisiting against the
cost of water in a specific deployment.

### Disease risk is not a diagnosis

The labels are produced by an agronomic rule engine with searched thresholds over moisture,
temperature, humidity and the wetness ratio. The output is a **stress-risk indicator**, not
a pathogen identification. It should be read as "conditions here favour disease" and used
to decide whether to inspect the crop, never as a diagnosis to act on.

## Data

### Weather: real

NASA POWER daily agroclimatology, 12 stations, 2015 to 2024, 43,836 daily records, cached
locally under `backend/ml/datasets/cache`. Provenance and licensing are in
[DATASETS.md](DATASETS.md).

ET0 is computed with FAO-56 Penman-Monteith, equation 6, and validated against nine
published reference examples in `backend/ml/tests/test_fao56.py`. Hargreaves is implemented
as a fallback for when no station is within range.

### Soil and crops: simulated

There are no field trials behind this, so the soil side is generated by forward simulation
rather than interpolated from measurements that do not exist.

A daily water balance carries each zone:

```
D(t+1) = max(0, D(t) + ETc * Ks * Ka - rainfall + irrigation - deep_percolation)
```

with crop coefficients by growth stage, soil-specific field capacity and wilting point, and
an infiltration model that determines how much of any application the soil can hold. The
balance is checked for closure at every step.

The result is 68,149 rows across 12 stations, four crops, three soil types and a range of
configurations.

### What this buys and what it costs

Simulation gives physically consistent features and full control over the label
distribution, which is what makes grouped evaluation possible at all. It also means the
model has never seen a real sensor, a real irrigation valve, or a real crop response. The
soil hydraulic parameters are literature values, not measurements of the field being
irrigated.

Treat the absolute numbers as a working demonstration of the approach, and treat the
pipeline as the deliverable. Calibration against field data is the natural next step and
the one that would make the absolute figures mean something.

## Evaluation

Three complementary splits, because any single one is misleading here:

| Split | Guards against |
| --- | --- |
| Grouped by station, crop and soil | The model memorising a configuration rather than learning the physics |
| Temporal holdout | Training on the future |
| Unseen station | Generalising to a climate never seen in training |

The unseen-station check is the honest one. A model that scores well on stations it has
seen and poorly on one it has not is fitting the weather record, not the irrigation problem.

The disease evaluation set is **degenerate**: the held-out window contains very few positive
cases, and one class has a support of 2. The macro F1 of 0.908 is reported because it is
what the model scored, and the training run flags it in its own summary. It is a smoke
signal, not a performance claim. This is stated on the dashboard's model page too, rather
than hidden.

## What the disease model can and cannot mean

The corpus labels are not observations. `backend/ml/dataset_builder.py` generates them by
running the rule engine in `artifacts/disease_thresholds.json` over six climate and soil
features, and `backend/ml/tests/test_disease_labels.py` confirms the stored column is
reproduced at **100%** from those six features alone.

Two consequences follow, and both are load-bearing:

1. The classifier can only learn a smooth approximation of a lookup table that ships with
   this repository. Its score measures memorisation, not agronomy.
2. A memorised table transfers perfectly, which is exactly why the unseen-station macro F1
   comes out at **1.0000**. A learned model essentially never does that. That number is an
   artefact of the labelling process and must never be quoted as generalisation.

There is no modelling fix here, because there is no disease signal in the data to learn
without real labelled observations. The honest options are to source a real dataset, or to
ship the rule engine directly as transparent reference logic and drop the classifier. Until
then this is a stress-risk indicator for the dashboard and nothing more, and the caveat is
carried in the bundle metadata, the `/api/health` provenance block and the training log so
it cannot be separated from the number.

Reproduce from `backend/ml/artifacts/metrics.json`.

| Estimator | Metric | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Irrigation decision | Average precision | 0.5334 |
| Irrigation decision | No-skill baseline (positive rate) | 0.0198 |
| Irrigation decision | Precision / recall, per-group thresholds | 0.449 / 0.722 |
| Irrigation decision | F1, end-to-end | 0.5499 |
| Irrigation decision | Calibration ECE / slope | 0.0044 / 1.20 |
| Irrigation decision | Seed spread over 3 refits | 0.3674 ± 0.0148 |
| Water depth | MAE / R² | 1.0532 mm / 0.9630 |
| Water depth | 90% conformal half-width | ±2.25 mm (86% achieved) |
| Water depth OOD | R², unseen station | 0.0816 |
| Water depth OOD | F1, unseen station | 0.7031 |
| Nutrient N | MAE | 7.86 kg/ha |
| Nutrient P | MAE | 2.88 kg/ha |
| Nutrient K | MAE | 3.62 kg/ha |
| Yield | MAE / R² | 0.2497 t/ha / 0.9677 |

Three of these deserve explanation, because they are the numbers that were previously
reported in a way that flattered the system.

**Accuracy is not reported for the decision.** 97.9% of rows need no water, so a model that
never irrigates anything scores 0.979. Average precision is used instead, with the positive
rate quoted beside it as the no-skill baseline: 0.5334 against 0.0198 is 26.9x lift, and
that is a claim worth making. The old F1 0.5327 is a real but much less flattering framing
of the same predictions.

**The threshold is per-group and calibrated.** The score is isotonic-calibrated over grouped
folds, so the threshold is an event rate rather than an arbitrary cut. Eight of fifteen
crop-and-soil groups are tuned on their own data, spanning 0.09 (rice on sandy loam) to
0.27 (maize on clay loam). A group with too few rows falls back to the global threshold and
records `global_fallback` in the decision, and the explanation tells the operator the
recommendation is less trustworthy. On the irrigate class this moved F1 from 0.537 to 0.554
and recall from 0.663 to 0.722.

**The depth interval does not hit its target.** It promises 90% coverage and achieves 86%
on held-out events. The residuals are out-of-fold, grouped by series, so the width is honest
rather than fitted; a marginal gap is expected and the shortfall is reported next to the
claim. An earlier version calibrated on training residuals and achieved 82% against the same
90% promise, which is the failure mode this guards against.

**The unseen-station depth R² is poor** — 0.0816, against 0.963 in-distribution. A
station-level multiplicative correction is fitted and reported both with and without
(`water_depth_ood_uncorrected` in `metrics.json`), but on this holdout the factor came out
at 0.998, so the correction is a no-op and the gap is variance rather than offset. It is
recorded as `correction_helped: false`. The decision itself still transfers at F1 0.7031,
which is the number that matters operationally.

## Reproducing

```bat
cd backend
..\.venv\Scripts\python ml\scripts\train.py --skip-tuning
```

Omit `--skip-tuning` to search the rule-engine thresholds. Add `--seed-stability` to refit
the decision and depth models over three seeds and report a spread instead of a single
number. Full training takes about 40 seconds on 20 threads, or roughly 6 minutes with
`--seed-stability`.

Every run writes the bundle, `metrics.json`, the training log and figures to
`backend/ml/artifacts/`, and appends a versioned entry to `backend/ml/artifacts/registry/`
containing the headline metrics and a delta against the previous run. A retrain used to
overwrite the only artefact the backend serves, so a worse run could not be rolled back;
now the last five bundles are retained on disk. The registry is gitignored because each
entry is several MB and the bloat belongs in history, not in a clone.

## Operating limits worth knowing

- **Drift monitoring.** `GET /api/models/drift` compares live readings against decile edges
  recorded at training time, by population stability index. Below 0.1 is negligible, 0.1 to
  0.25 warrants investigation, above 0.25 means retrain. It returns `null` rather than a
  number when there are too few readings, and flags a frozen probe separately from drift,
  since a stuck sensor is a hardware fault and would otherwise dominate the score.
- **The server is unauthenticated.** Field-local only. Anyone who can reach the port can
  open a valve.
- **No field validation.** Every number here comes from a physically simulated corpus driven
  by NASA POWER weather. Nothing has been checked against a real crop.

Verify the ET0 implementation independently at any time:

```bat
..\.venv\Scripts\python ml\tests\test_fao56.py
```

## Serving

`ml/inference.py` is the only inference path, and it reads the exact column order each
estimator was trained on from `model_columns` in the bundle. That field is recorded at
training time rather than reconstructed at serve time, because the training frame
assembles the one-hot groups in dataframe column order, which differs from the declared
feature order. Reconstructing it produced a matrix scikit-learn rejected; recording it
cannot drift.

The bundle is loaded once at startup. If it is missing, the system falls back to
rule-based decisions and says so in the response, rather than failing to control.
